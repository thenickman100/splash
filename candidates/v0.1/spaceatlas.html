Space Atlas

A virtual experience about the future of space, made at the Air Force Academy for Newt Gingrich.

Started2018
Addressthespaceatlas.org
Commissioned byNewt Gingrich, via USAFA

Assignment

Around 2017, Newt Gingrich, the former Speaker of the U.S. House of Representatives and a longtime advocate of space exploration, commissioned the U.S. Air Force Academy to create a virtual experience that would teach the future of space, looking ahead to 2038. My best friend at USAFA was in the Astronautical Engineering department, and leading the team became his capstone project. I wasn't an Astronautical Engineering major, but he knew the Home start page I had built for myself. He was impressed with it, and he picked me to lead the web design.

Development

I worked most closely with our graphic artist, who designed assets for the site. My best friend and others on the team made the 3D renders in Blender, the rest did research, and another cadet took over as lead in the second semester. The project was technically very demanding, and with my friend's leadership we managed to pull it off. The whole site was a challenge, and when it was finished it felt like a masterpiece. We presented the work to the department and at Space Symposium, and we collaborated with SpaceX, Lockheed Martin, and JPL. I'm not sure how far the project went beyond that.

Earth, the Moon, and Mars

Pick Earth, the Moon, or Mars, drag to spin it, and tap a marker to open one of 13 pages, from the Big Falcon Rocket, New Glenn, and Vulcan to a commercial space station, the Lunar Gateway, and bases on the Moon and Mars. The renders are large and take a moment to load. Each planet and spacecraft is a sequence of 80 Blender renders, and dragging flips through the frames, so the object appears to turn while the browser only displays flat images.
