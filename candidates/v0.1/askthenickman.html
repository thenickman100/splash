Ask a Ninja

I enjoyed the video podcast Ask a Ninja, where a ninja answers viewers' questions, and I thought it would be cool to make my own version. I was just a kid, so it never worked out. I made a few home videos with my sister for the site and uploaded some videos I made in classes at school.

Microsoft Word

I made the first pages in Microsoft Word, with WordArt and gradient shapes. Word could export a document to HTML, which sounded neat, so I figured out how to do it and uploaded the HTML to webs.com. That's why the logo says .WEBS.COM. For a while the site also lived at askthenickman.net23.net. Then my neighbor taught me how to register a domain, and he registered askthenickman.com for me.

Contents

I posted whatever I was into. That included Flash games, the secret phone numbers from The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy, one of those chat rooms where you talked with random strangers, and Fundiferous Nothing, comics my middle school friend Jackie drew for me, which are also in the Awesome Stuff photo albums. The Programming section started as a school project where I had to research something and present it to the class. I researched all the languages needed to make websites (HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and XML) and put what I learned on the site as lesson pages, one for each tag and topic.

Over time I posted more games than anything else, until Askthenickman was mostly a games site. In 2011 I moved the games to their own site, Hack a HOBO. The Askthenickman games page ended up loading its game menu from hackahobo.info, and it still has a commented-out line that once sent visitors straight to the new site.

Holidays

I enjoyed websites with holiday themes, and I wanted to try it myself. I made a themed logo for each holiday, each with its own copy of the home page, and added JavaScript effects to match. The Christmas page had falling snow, and other pages had fireworks or extra mouse cursors that trailed behind the real one.

Rebuilt site

Askthenickman, rebuilt from the webs.com version using the files I could find. The games list, the holiday pages, the programming lessons, and the “More” page all work. Some links go to pages that no longer exist.
