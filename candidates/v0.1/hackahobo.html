Hack a HOBO

The games site that started on Askthenickman. I still work on it today.

Started2011

Origins

When I moved the games from Askthenickman to their own site in 2011, my sister came up with the name. I wanted the site themed around cavemen, and I thought “hobo” was another word for a caveman. The “Hack” probably came from the hacking tutorials on Awesome Stuff. The first layout was based on Facebook's design in early 2011, with a blue bar across the top, small navigation buttons on the right, and content in boxes underneath. I started Hack a HOBO in middle school and kept it going through high school as a way for kids to get around blocked gaming sites at school.

Profiles

When I started learning PHP, I wanted to make my own social media site, so I added profiles to Hack a HOBO. People could sign up, log in, and have their own page. It was hard to prevent spam, injection attacks, and similar problems, so I removed the profiles. I never liked gaming sites that tried to force the social media aspect anyway, so that was an easy decision.

Hiatus

The original version ran from 2011 to 2015, and then the site went on hiatus. One of the main reasons was the death of Flash. Most of the games were Flash games. For years before it ended, browsers warned that Flash was going away and started blocking it by default, and Adobe finally shut it down at the end of 2020. Many of the other games ran on Shockwave and Java, which browsers dropped too. In 2024, Flash was just starting to be revived through Ruffle, and I started on the 2024 Edition.

2024 and 2026 Editions

The 2024 Edition made the site modern, so it works in current browsers and on phones, and it brought the games back. The site now runs Flash games with Ruffle, Java games with CheerpJ, and Shockwave games with DirPlayer. Only a handful of the Flash games still aren't fully working in Ruffle. DirPlayer only took off in the past year, and soon almost every Shockwave game will be fully playable. Previews of its latest GitHub build show near-perfect emulation of the complicated 3D games of the past.

The 2026 Edition was an overhaul to fix design problems that had been on the site for over a decade, which I finally had ideas and fixes for. The first fix was the left sidebar, which now holds a featured game and the navigation tools. The second was game containers on the home page, which let me give the site custom holiday themes. The holidays I most wanted were Halloween and Christmas, and the containers made those themes possible by letting me use more colors. I've always wanted snow to collect on the game containers, and I plan to have that working by Christmas 2027.

Golden age, November 6, 2012

The site at its peak, rebuilt from archived copies. The chat box shows a real chat log, with offensive and personal messages removed. The forms no longer send anything.
